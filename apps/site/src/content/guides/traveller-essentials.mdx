Most courts in Malaysia are easy to play on once you know how booking works. The hard part for visitors is usually not the tennis. It is the phone number, the payment method, or a booking site in Malay.

## Get a Malaysian number or eSIM

Many venues take bookings over WhatsApp, and some booking portals send a one-time code by SMS to a Malaysian (+60) number. A local prepaid SIM or eSIM bought at the airport solves both. Your home WhatsApp still works for messaging venues; the +60 number matters mainly for SMS codes.

## Have a way to pay locally

- **Cash** is still common at council and federal sports complexes. Some book online but take payment only at the counter.
- **FPX** is Malaysian online banking. Portals that only accept FPX usually will not work with a foreign bank card.
- **Touch 'n Go eWallet** and **DuitNow QR** are widely accepted at private venues.
- **Cards** work at most commercial courts, clubs and hotels.

## Play early or late

Midday is hot and humid. Most players book before 9am or after 5pm, which is also when courts are busiest. Floodlit courts often charge a higher night rate, typically 1.3 to 2 times the day rate.

## Plan around the rain

Afternoon thunderstorms are common all year. The east coast and Sabah get their monsoon from November to March. If rain is likely, look for [covered courts](/indoor-tennis-courts/), and ask the venue about their rain policy before you pay.

## Dress code and kit

Private clubs usually require proper tennis clothing and non-marking shoes, and some do not allow sleeveless shirts. Council courts are relaxed. Racquet and ball hire is rare outside academies and hotels, so bring your own.

## Can I play at a private court?

Often, yes, with the right invitation. Each listing on this site tells you who can play. See the guides for [clubs](/guides/club-visitor/), [hotels](/guides/hotel-non-guest/), [condominiums](/guides/condo-guest/) and [universities](/guides/university-courts/).
